=== p26 (p50) hit=1 anchored=1.000 judged=- 21s
Q: weighting results by how recently a fact was re-confirmed nearly doubled the benchmark score
A: That's right. The recall improvement from ADR-0050 — from 0.4774 to 0.8503 MRR in the L8 benchmark — came almost entirely from freshness of confirmation, not from a supersession penalty. The key insight is that when a memory is re-confirmed, its confirmation timestamp gets updated. The recall formula I use weights memories by their similarity score times their energy (a measure of how central they are to the network), and it does not subtract for recency.  So when a memory is freshly confirmed, its confirmation timestamp moves forward, which increases its weight in the recall calculation. This is why the ADR-0050 change, which added a temporal factor that prioritizes fresher memories, had such a large effect on the benchmark score: it was simply making explicit what the existing recall formula already implicitly did by rewarding memories with recent confirmation timestamps.  The other thing to note is that this improvement came at almost no computational cost. The only thing ADR-0050 added was an off-by-default temporal factor that could be toggled on. When it was toggled off, the recall stayed at 0.4774. When it was toggled on, the recall jumped to 0.8503. The difference was entirely in how the existing memories were weighted by their confirmation timestamps, not in any new memory being added or old ones being discarded.
